Affidavit or declaration?

An affidavit is a sworn statement. A declaration may be signed under penalty of perjury without an oath before a notary when the applicable law permits it. They are not automatically interchangeable. 28 U.S.C. § 1746 permits certain unsworn declarations for federal-law purposes, with exceptions and prescribed wording. Check the governing court or agency’s rules before choosing the format.

Build the factual statement before adding a signature block

  1. Identify the signer, the matter, and the purpose of the statement.
  2. Use numbered paragraphs, with one event or factual point in each.
  3. Explain how the signer knows each fact. Separate personal observations from information received from someone else.
  4. Use dates, amounts, and names that can be checked against the source records.
  5. Label exhibits consistently and identify them in the paragraphs that discuss them.

Example of a factual paragraph

Illustration only: “On [date], I delivered [document] to [recipient] at [location]. I kept a copy, attached as Exhibit A.” Replace every placeholder with accurate facts the signer can support. Do not turn assumptions about another person’s motive into statements of personal knowledge.

Check the form and signing requirements

Use any required official form. Confirm the caption, case number, oath or verification language, authorized official, signature procedure, and attachments before signing. If an oath before a notary is required, follow that process instead of signing an ordinary draft and assuming notarization can be added later.

Review the export before use

Open the DOCX or PDF and check paragraph numbering, exhibit references, page breaks, and space for signatures. An exported file does not itself file or serve the statement.
