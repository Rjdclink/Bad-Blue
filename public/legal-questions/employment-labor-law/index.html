Employment & Labor Law Legal Questions Online

Use LegalWhat to work through employment & labor law questions in a continuing voice or text conversation. Start with what happened, add the jurisdiction and important dates, and upload the controlling document when the answer depends on exact wording.

Start the Conversation

Start with the workplace issue you need answered

For a pay dispute, compare the hours you actually worked with your time records and pay statements. For termination or discipline, write down the employer’s stated reason, the date you learned of the decision, and any earlier complaints or accommodation requests. Keep the original messages alongside your timeline.

Which facts change the research?

  • Where you worked, including remote-work locations, and whether the employer is private or a government agency.
  • Your employee or contractor classification, job duties, pay arrangement, and any union agreement.
  • The specific action you are questioning, who made the decision, and when it happened.
  • What you reported or requested before the action, and the employer’s response.

Do not confuse an internal complaint with an agency filing

Reporting a problem to HR is not the same as filing a discrimination charge. The EEOC explains that an internal grievance or mediation generally does not extend its charge-filing deadline. Federal employees have a different process. Check the applicable deadline promptly instead of assuming that an ongoing workplace discussion preserves it.

Turn your records into a focused question

For example: “I worked in [state], reported [specific concern] on [date], and received [discipline or termination] on [date]. Which rules and filing procedures should I check?” This separates the sequence of events from conclusions that still need evidence.

Official starting point

Read the EEOC’s charge-filing time limits and identify the relevant state or local agency for the particular claim. Wage, contract, and discrimination disputes may follow different routes.
