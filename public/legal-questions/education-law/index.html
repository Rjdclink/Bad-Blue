Education Law Legal Questions Online

Use LegalWhat to work through education law questions in a continuing voice or text conversation. Start with what happened, add the jurisdiction and important dates, and upload the controlling document when the answer depends on exact wording.

Start the Conversation

Identify the school decision you want to understand

A suspension notice, denied accommodation, disputed evaluation, and refusal to provide records call for different questions. Start with the actual notice or written decision, the school or district, the student’s grade or program, and the date the family received it.

Build a school-record packet

  • Keep the current IEP or Section 504 plan, evaluations, and prior versions relevant to the disagreement.
  • Collect notices, meeting invitations, attendance records, disciplinary reports, and written requests.
  • List the support requested, what the school agreed to do, and what happened afterward.
  • Record upcoming meetings and any response or appeal dates printed on the notice.

Keep the procedures separate

A school appeal, special-education dispute, records request, and civil-rights complaint are not interchangeable. Ask which process addresses the particular decision and whether starting one affects another. Do not assume a conversation with a teacher or principal starts a formal review.

Where to check special-education rights

The U.S. Department of Education’s IDEA resources for parents and families explain procedural safeguards and provide links to dispute-resolution information. Obtain the current safeguards notice from your school and check the state education agency’s procedures. IDEA resources do not cover every dispute in every school setting.

A useful question to bring to the conversation

“My child’s school issued [decision] on [date]. The current plan says [specific provision], and these records show [events]. Which procedure addresses this disagreement, and what documents should I prepare?” Keep the requested outcome clear, such as an evaluation, meeting, records access, or review of a decision.
