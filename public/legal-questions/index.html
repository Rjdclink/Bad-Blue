Start with the problem you actually have

You do not need to decide in advance whether your issue is “procedural,” “contractual,” “constitutional,” or another legal category. Start with the event, document, deadline, or decision you are dealing with. The area pages below provide a more focused place to continue once the subject is clear.

Examples of useful question types

  • Meaning: “What does this court order or contract clause require?”
  • Deadline: “What date may control my response, appeal, notice, or filing?”
  • Procedure: “What usually happens next in this type of case or agency matter?”
  • Research: “What statutes, rules, cases, or official sources should I check for this issue?”
  • Evidence: “Which facts or documents matter most to this claim, defense, or dispute?”
  • Document: “What type of response, motion, letter, agreement, or form may fit the situation?”

What to include when the answer matters

Give the jurisdiction, important dates, current procedural stage, the exact source document when available, and the result you are trying to understand. If a fact is disputed or uncertain, say so. That keeps the conversation from treating an assumption as established fact.
