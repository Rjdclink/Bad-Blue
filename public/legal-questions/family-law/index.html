Family Law Legal Questions Online

Use LegalWhat to work through family law questions in a continuing voice or text conversation. Start with what happened, add the jurisdiction and important dates, and upload the controlling document when the answer depends on exact wording.

Start the Conversation

Begin with the current court order

If a case already exists, gather the signed decree, later modifications, parenting orders, and any agreements incorporated into them. Identify the court and case number. Quote the paragraph you need explained rather than relying on a memory of what was decided.

Separate enforcement, modification, and a new request

Ask whether you want someone to comply with an existing order, want the court to change an order, or need relief that has not been addressed. These are different questions. List the exact result you want and the facts supporting it; do not assume the same filing fits all three.

Prepare records for the issue

  • For parenting disputes: the ordered schedule, a dated account of exchanges, and relevant communications.
  • For support: payment history, income records, existing calculations, and notices of changed circumstances.
  • For property: the decree’s allocation, account statements, valuations, loan balances, and records of payments or transfers.
  • For procedural questions: service papers, hearing notices, and any mediation or dispute-resolution provision.

Questions to resolve before drafting

Which court can hear the request? Does the order require a step before filing? Is there a deadline, required form, or supporting financial disclosure? A move to another state does not by itself answer those questions. Check the responsible court’s current instructions.

Find the court’s own resources

Use your state judiciary’s official self-help and forms pages. The California Courts website, for example, links to its Self-Help Guide; those materials describe California procedures and should not be applied as another state’s rules.
