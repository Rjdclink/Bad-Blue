How Lexara Works

1. Talk naturally by voice or text

Lexara is LegalWhat's conversational legal AI. Users can explain a legal problem naturally by voice or text rather than learning a research interface first.

2. Work across 40 areas of law

The platform organizes legal guidance across 40 areas of law while preserving the ability to identify related issues that cross practice-area boundaries.

3. Resolve the legal context

Legal questions can turn on jurisdiction, procedural posture, deadlines, and facts. Lexara is designed to use the jurisdiction provided in the conversation and to identify uncertainty when a controlling jurisdiction or material fact has not been established.

4. Research current authority when the question requires it

Authority-sensitive questions can invoke LegalWhat's legal-research workflow, which prefers official court, legislature, government, regulatory, court-rule, and agency material when available. Retrieved material is evidence for analysis, not an automatic declaration that a source controls.

5. Use specialized AI capabilities together

LegalWhat uses multi-model orchestration. The system selects capabilities such as legal analysis, verification, research, reasoning, multimodal analysis, and structured output according to the task instead of depending on one model for every job.

6. Analyze uploaded material

Supported documents and media can be extracted or transcribed for downstream legal analysis. The extraction stage is instructed to preserve observable evidence, mark uncertainty, and avoid deciding credibility, admissibility, liability, or guilt merely from extraction.

7. Create documents when the objective calls for one

Lexara can identify when a legal document is appropriate to the user's objective and route the user into LegalWhat's document workflow. Supported documents can be generated for download as DOCX or PDF, with missing facts kept explicit rather than silently invented.

8. Keep uncertainty visible

LegalWhat is designed not to invent controlling law, cases, citations, deadlines, documents, or evidence when the required information is unavailable. Legal research and generated documents should be reviewed in light of the current law, facts, and applicable jurisdiction.
