Prepare Before an Attorney Consultation

Legal technology can be useful even when you know you need an attorney. Organizing the matter beforehand can help you explain the facts clearly, identify the documents that matter, and use the consultation time for the questions that require professional judgment.

Start Legal Research

Build a short factual timeline

List the key events in date order, including notices, filings, payments, communications, arrests, hearings, deadlines, or other events relevant to the problem.

Gather the source documents

Bring contracts, court papers, notices, correspondence, photographs, records, agency letters, and any prior legal documents that the attorney may need to review.

Write the questions that need professional judgment

Separate basic factual or procedural questions from strategic questions about risk, settlement, representation, privilege, litigation, or other matters where an attorney’s individualized advice is most valuable.

Avoid treating preliminary research as the final answer

Your preparation should make the professional consultation more efficient, not substitute for counsel when the matter requires legal representation or individualized advice.
