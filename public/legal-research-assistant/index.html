Legal Research Assistant

Legal research is most useful when it starts with a focused question and the correct jurisdiction. LegalWhat can help organize the issue, identify the kinds of authority that matter, and connect statutes, rules, cases, and source documents to the facts you provide.

Start Legal Research

Start with the legal question

Instead of collecting cases or statutes at random, define what needs to be answered: the elements of a claim, a deadline, a defense, a procedure, an evidentiary rule, a standard of review, or another concrete issue.

Use the right authority hierarchy

Constitutions, statutes, regulations, court rules, binding cases, persuasive cases, and secondary explanations do not carry the same weight. Research should distinguish them and prefer controlling primary authority when available.

Keep jurisdiction and date in view

The same question may have different answers across states, federal circuits, agencies, or courts. Authority can also change over time, so dates and current status matter.

Tie authority back to the facts

Research is not complete merely because a relevant rule was found. The useful step is explaining how that authority relates to the actual facts, procedural posture, documents, and questions in the matter.
