Legal Issue Spotting

People often know what happened but not what legal questions to ask. Issue spotting means organizing the facts into possible legal topics so the research can focus on the rules that may actually matter.

Start Legal Research

Separate facts from conclusions

Start with who did what, when, where, what documents exist, and what happened next. Labels such as fraud, harassment, breach, or discrimination may eventually be relevant, but the underlying facts should be identified first.

Look for procedure as well as substance

A strong legal analysis considers not only what rights or duties may exist, but also jurisdiction, standing, deadlines, exhaustion, notice, service, evidence, and the procedural vehicle for raising the issue.

Identify competing interpretations

The same facts may support more than one legal theory or defense. Issue spotting should surface plausible alternatives rather than forcing every problem into the first category that seems familiar.

Use the issues to direct research

Once the possible issues are identified, statutes, rules, cases, forms, and documents can be researched in a more targeted way.
