Case Law Research Assistant

Case law research is more than finding decisions that contain the same words as your question. The useful task is finding decisions from the right courts, understanding what legal issue they actually decided, and determining how closely their facts and procedural posture match your matter.

Start Legal Research

Find the right court hierarchy

A decision may be binding, persuasive, or irrelevant depending on the court and jurisdiction. Research should identify the controlling court system before relying on a case.

Read beyond the headline

Summaries and snippets can hide important limits. The holding, procedural posture, standard of review, factual distinctions, and later treatment of the case may all matter.

Compare facts and legal issues

Two cases can discuss the same doctrine but reach different results because the facts are different. Good research identifies which factual differences the court considered legally important.

Check current validity

Later cases, legislation, rule changes, or higher-court decisions can limit or overrule older authority. Important cases should be checked for current treatment before being relied upon.

A case-research worksheet

  1. Write the question. Identify the disputed rule, relevant facts, jurisdiction, and procedural stage.
  2. Record the decision. Save its full name, citation or docket number, court, date, and a link to the actual opinion.
  3. Find the passage. Note the page containing the rule and the reasoning that connects it to the result.
  4. Compare the facts. List similarities and differences instead of assuming a matching keyword means a matching case.
  5. Check later treatment. Look for later opinions, amendments, rehearing orders, and controlling authority that may change the rule.

Where to retrieve federal decisions

The federal judiciary’s Find a Case resource describes access to federal cases and court opinions. A docket entry, a party’s brief, and a judicial opinion are different kinds of documents. Confirm which one you are reading. For state cases, begin with the issuing court’s official opinion collection.

Example of narrowing a search

“Contract case” is broad. A more useful starting question is “What rule does the controlling court apply to [specific contract issue] at [procedural stage]?” Add the jurisdiction and relevant facts, then test the results against the complete opinion. Finding a decision does not establish that it remains good law.
