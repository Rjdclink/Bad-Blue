Sources, Jurisdiction & Accuracy

Legal answers depend on jurisdiction

Lexara is designed to identify the jurisdiction supplied in the conversation and to treat jurisdiction as material when researching legal rules, deadlines, procedure, and authority. A source from another jurisdiction is not treated as binding merely because it discusses a similar issue.

Primary authority comes first

For authority-sensitive legal questions, LegalWhat's research workflow is designed to prefer current primary material such as constitutions, statutes, regulations, court rules, official court opinions, and official agency material. Secondary sources can provide context and research leads, but they are not automatically controlling authority.

Retrieval is evidence, not proof

A retrieved page or citation is not treated as controlling merely because a search system found it. Lexara's legal-analysis instructions require the system to distinguish controlling from persuasive material, identify jurisdiction or effective-date uncertainty, avoid inventing citations or holdings, and avoid claiming that unprovided evidence was reviewed.

Multiple-model cross-validation

LegalWhat uses a capability-based multi-model orchestration system for legal analysis, verification, reasoning, research, multimodal work, and structured output. Models are selected for the task rather than treated as interchangeable votes. Agreement between models does not replace verification against legal authority.

What LegalWhat does not promise

LegalWhat does not claim that AI is infallible, that every retrieved authority is controlling, or that a generated answer is automatically ready for filing or reliance without review. Legal rules can change and factual differences can change outcomes.

Practical standard: identify the jurisdiction, prefer current primary authority, distinguish binding from persuasive material, expose uncertainty, and never manufacture a source.
