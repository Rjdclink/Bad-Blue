Affordable Legal Research Help

Legal research can become expensive when the question is still broad. LegalWhat can help narrow the issue, identify the governing jurisdiction, organize the sources to look for, and connect the research to the facts and documents in your matter.

Start LegalWhat

Narrow the question first

Research is more useful when it begins with a specific question: what rule applies, what deadline controls, what elements must be shown, what procedure comes next, or what authority supports a particular argument.

Research the right jurisdiction

A case or statute from the wrong jurisdiction may be interesting but not controlling. LegalWhat is designed to focus research around the court, state, agency, or other legal system relevant to the user’s facts.

Separate primary authority from explanation

Summaries can make the law easier to understand, but important conclusions should be checked against current statutes, regulations, court rules, cases, and official instructions whenever possible.

Connect research to the matter

Research should not exist in isolation. The useful question is how the authority relates to the user’s timeline, documents, procedural posture, and desired outcome.
