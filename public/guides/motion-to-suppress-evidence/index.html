Motion to Suppress Evidence

Suppression analysis is fact-specific. Organize the timeline, who acted, what was searched or seized, whether a warrant existed, what it authorized, statements made, and controlling jurisdiction.

Search concepts: motion to suppress evidence; suppress illegal search; Fourth Amendment motion; suppress statement.

Facts to organize first

Build a clear timeline of the stop, search, seizure, questioning, identification procedure, or other challenged event. Note who acted, where it happened, whether a warrant or consent was involved, what was taken or said, what documents exist, and which court or jurisdiction controls. Small factual differences can change which legal questions matter.

Research before filing

Check current constitutional authority, statutes, rules of evidence and criminal procedure, local motion requirements, filing deadlines, and controlling appellate decisions. Compare the exact facts with the authorities rather than assuming a general rule applies. If a warrant, video, report, transcript, or statement is important, review the source material itself and confirm that citations and quotations are accurate.
