How to Organize a Legal Problem

A legal problem is easier to research when the facts are organized. You do not need a perfect legal theory; you need a clear timeline, the relevant people or organizations, the jurisdiction, source documents, and the questions you want answered.

Ask a Legal Question

Build the timeline

Write the key events in order with dates where possible. Include communications, notices, payments, filings, arrests, hearings, agreements, injuries, or other events that may affect the issue.

List the parties and roles

Identify who is involved and how they relate to the matter: employer, landlord, agency, court, creditor, business, officer, family member, contractor, or other participant.

Gather the documents

Collect contracts, messages, orders, notices, receipts, filings, photographs, reports, and other records that may confirm the timeline or contain exact language.

Write the questions separately

Once the facts are organized, list the specific things you need to understand. This helps prevent the most important question from being buried inside the background story.
