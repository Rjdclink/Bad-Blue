Review and Revise a Legal Document

Drafting is only one part of document work. LegalWhat can help review a supported document, explain its structure, identify points that deserve attention, and assist with revisions while preserving the user’s underlying purpose.

Start a Document Conversation

Review facts before style

Names, dates, amounts, addresses, case numbers, party roles, attachments, and factual assertions should be checked before focusing on wording. A beautifully written error is still an error.

Check structure and completeness

Depending on the document, review headings, defined terms, requested relief, signature blocks, exhibits, service language, governing law, or required sections. Missing structure can matter as much as unclear prose.

Check jurisdiction and authority

If the document cites law, follows a court rule, or is intended for filing, verify that the cited authority is current and applicable to the correct jurisdiction.

Revise without changing the intended meaning accidentally

Edits can alter obligations, admissions, deadlines, remedies, or legal positions. Review substantive changes carefully and obtain professional review when the document creates significant legal or financial consequences.
