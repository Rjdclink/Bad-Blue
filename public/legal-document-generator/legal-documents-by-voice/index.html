Create Legal Documents by Voice

If typing a long factual history is inconvenient, you can begin document preparation by speaking. Voice is useful for explaining the situation; exact text, names, numbers, and source documents can still be added when precision matters.

Start a Document Conversation

Start with the story

Explain who is involved, what happened, what you want the document to accomplish, and whether there is already a case, contract, notice, order, or deadline.

Let follow-up questions structure the draft

A document conversation can identify missing parties, dates, requested relief, governing terms, attachments, jurisdiction, or procedural details before the draft is assembled.

Use text for exact language

Voice is convenient, but exact names, addresses, dollar amounts, statutory citations, quoted contract terms, docket numbers, and signature information should be confirmed in text or source documents.

Review the finished language

A voice-driven workflow still ends with a written legal document. Read the entire draft and verify the legal and factual details before relying on it.
