Incarceration and Inmate Search

When you need to know whether a person is publicly listed as incarcerated or where they are being held, the strongest sources are usually the responsible correctional agency, federal inmate locator, state department of corrections, or county jail roster.

Start a Research Conversation

Use public-record research responsibly. LegalWhat is designed to organize and explain lawful public-source information. Availability, completeness, identity matching, and record status can vary by source and jurisdiction.

Search by custody level

Federal prisons, state prisons, county jails, detention centers, and local holding facilities may use separate systems. Knowing the likely jurisdiction can dramatically narrow the search.

Use multiple identity fields

Full name, age or birth year, register or inmate number, sentencing jurisdiction, and prior location can help distinguish people with similar names.

Custody data changes

Transfers, releases, temporary holds, and processing delays can make one source stale. Treat the most recent official custody source as more authoritative than older secondary listings.

Distinguish custody from conviction

Being listed in a jail or detention system does not by itself establish guilt or a final conviction. Public custody information should be described accurately and separately from the underlying case disposition.
