Court Record Research

Court records can answer questions about filed cases, procedural history, publicly available orders, docket activity, and litigation involving a person or organization. LegalWhat can help identify the right court system and organize what the public record shows.

Start a Research Conversation

Use public-record research responsibly. LegalWhat is designed to organize and explain lawful public-source information. Availability, completeness, identity matching, and record status can vary by source and jurisdiction.

Start with the court and jurisdiction

Federal, state, county, municipal, bankruptcy, appellate, and specialty courts use different systems. A useful search begins by identifying the jurisdiction and likely court rather than treating all court records as one database.

Match the correct party

Names may be shared by many people or businesses. Location, middle name, filing date, case type, attorney, company affiliation, or other public details can help determine whether a record actually belongs to the subject being researched.

Read the docket carefully

A docket entry can show that something was filed, but it does not necessarily prove what happened on the merits. Distinguish allegations, motions, orders, dismissals, judgments, appeals, and final dispositions.

Use the record in legal research

When a public case is relevant to a current legal question, the docket and available filings can be combined with statute, rule, and case-law research to understand the procedural and legal context.
