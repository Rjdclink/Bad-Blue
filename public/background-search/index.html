Background and Public Records Search

LegalWhat can help research publicly available background information when it is relevant to a legal question or when you directly ask for a public-source fact. The goal is a concise answer supported by the strongest available official or primary records.

Start a Research Conversation

Use public-record research responsibly. LegalWhat is designed to organize and explain lawful public-source information. Availability, completeness, identity matching, and record status can vary by source and jurisdiction.

Search the source that fits the question

Different questions belong in different sources. Court-record questions should prioritize courts and docket systems; incarceration questions should prioritize correctional agencies and jail rosters; licensing questions should prioritize the relevant state licensing board; business questions should prioritize state entity registries.

Identity matching matters

A name alone is often not enough. City, state, middle name, age range, employer, license type, case location, or other non-sensitive clues can help separate people with similar names and reduce false matches.

Official sources first

Where possible, LegalWhat should prefer official court, agency, licensing, correctional, property, and business-registry sources, then use reputable secondary sources to fill gaps or provide context.

Direct answers with source awareness

For a focused question such as whether a public license exists or where someone is publicly listed as incarcerated, the useful result is a short answer with enough source context to understand how the conclusion was reached.
