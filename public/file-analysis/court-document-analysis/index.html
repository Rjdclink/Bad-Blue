Court Document Analysis

Court papers can be difficult to read because important information is spread across captions, procedural language, citations, factual allegations, deadlines, and requested relief. LegalWhat can help organize those pieces into a clearer explanation.

Analyze a Legal File

Identify what kind of document it is

An order, motion, complaint, summons, brief, notice, judgment, and docket entry each serve different purposes. Understanding the document type is the first step toward understanding what it means.

Pull out the procedural information

Case number, court, parties, filing date, hearing date, response deadline, service information, and requested relief can determine what should happen next.

Separate allegations from rulings

Statements in a complaint or motion are not the same as findings by a court. The analysis should distinguish what a party claims, what another party argues, and what the judge actually ordered or decided.

Research cited authority and rules

If a filing cites statutes, rules, or cases, those sources can be researched separately to understand whether the filing accurately describes the governing law.
