Analyze a Legal Notice or Demand Letter

Formal notices often create deadlines or demand a response. LegalWhat can help identify who sent the notice, what it claims, what action is requested, what dates matter, and which legal questions should be researched before responding.

Analyze a Legal File

Identify the sender and authority

Determine whether the notice came from a court, agency, attorney, landlord, creditor, employer, business, or other party, and whether it cites a contract, statute, rule, order, or policy.

Extract deadlines and requested action

Payment dates, cure periods, hearing dates, response deadlines, termination dates, appeal periods, and document-production deadlines can be easy to miss in dense correspondence.

Separate facts from legal assertions

A letter can state a legal position forcefully without necessarily being correct. Verify important legal claims against the actual contract, statute, rule, or order.

Plan the response

The appropriate response may be a letter, filing, payment, request for information, administrative appeal, negotiation, or consultation with counsel. The document’s source and jurisdiction will help determine the next step.
