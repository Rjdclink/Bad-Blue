Legal Evidence Analysis

Evidence analysis begins with the factual question the evidence is supposed to prove or disprove. LegalWhat can help organize supported evidence, identify relevant details, and connect those details to the legal issues being researched.

Analyze a Legal File

Identify the proposition

For each item, ask what fact it tends to support: identity, timing, notice, payment, injury, possession, intent, communication, location, damages, or another disputed point.

Keep source and context intact

A screenshot, excerpt, photograph, video frame, message, or record can be misleading when separated from its source or surrounding context. Preserve metadata and original files when possible.

Separate relevance from admissibility

Something may seem relevant but still face authentication, hearsay, foundation, privilege, chain-of-custody, or other evidentiary issues. Those are separate legal questions that may require jurisdiction-specific research.

Build an evidence map

Organizing each piece of evidence by disputed fact, source, date, witness, and legal issue can make gaps and contradictions easier to see.
